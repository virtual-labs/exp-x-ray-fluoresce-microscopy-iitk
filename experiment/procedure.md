<b> Methodology:</b><br><br>

1.  Sample Irradiation and Initial Beam Formation : <br>
a.	The sample is irradiated with an X-ray source.<br>
b.	The emitted X-ray fluorescence from the sample is directed towards collimators.<br><br>

2.	Collimation :<br>
a.	Collimators are used to control the divergence of the X-ray beam, ensuring a parallel or near-parallel beam.<br>
b.	The collimated X-ray beam is directed onto an analyzing crystal.<br>
c.	A selection of different crystals (e.g., LiF, Ge, pentaerythritol, thallium acid phthalate) as mentioned in Table 1, is used to cover a wide range of X-ray wavelengths.<br>
d.	Crystal fluorescence is also used for background noise reduction.<br>
e.	The analyzing crystal disperses the X-rays according to Bragg's Law, separating them into their constituent wavelengths.<br><br>

3.	Diffracted Beam Detection and result :<br>
a.	A detector receives the diffracted X-ray beam from the analyzing crystal.<br>
b.	The detector converts the received X-ray radiation into measurable voltage pulses.<br>
c.	The voltage pulses are processed and converted into a pulse height distribution (PHD) plot, which represents the intensity of X-rays at different energies as shown in Fig 2.<br>
d.	Identification of X-rays peaks for the determination of chemical composition of materials and the intensity of peaks provide the information about the concentration of composition of material.<br><br>

Table 1: List of crystals used for detection of wide variety of materials (metals, alloys and ceramics)<br>

| Crystals used for analyzing crystal   | Energy of X-rays                                              |
|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| LiF (Lithium fluoride)                | Very short to medium energy (nickel, copper, Zinc)            |
| Ge (Germanium)                        | For higher energy x-rays (titanium, vanadium, etc.)           |
| PE (Pentaerythritol)                  | For medium energy x-rays (chlorine, sulfur, phosphorus)       |
| TIAP (Thallium acid phthalate)        | For light energy x-rays (oxygen, carbon, nitrogen)            |
| Crystal Fluorescence                  | For background noises                                         |
